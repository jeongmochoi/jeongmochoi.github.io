## Course Overview

<dl class="course-meta-list">
  <dt>Course Code</dt>
  <dd>CH70445</dd>

  <dt>Course Description</dt>
  <dd>This graduate-level course will focus on how to model chemical and biological problems with the tools from statistical mechanics. The discussion will start from the foundations of statistical mechanics, and will move on to the applications to basic thermodynamics. In the second half of the course, the systems with interactions will be discussed with a strong emphasis on chemical and biological applications, such as bio- and nano-machines, water, and polymers.</dd>

  <dt>Time and Classroom</dt>
  <dd>Mon 4:30–7:30 pm, Room 409</dd>

  <dt>Textbooks</dt>
  <dd><ul>
      <li>Main: Ken A. Dill and Sarina Bromberg, <em>Molecular Driving Forces</em>, 2nd Ed., Garland Science, 2011, ISBN 9780815344308</li>
      <li>Auxiliary: Donald A. McQuarrie, <em>Statistical Mechanics</em>, University Science Books, 2000, ISBN 9781891389153</li>
    </ul></dd>

  <dt>Prerequisite</dt>
  <dd>Undergraduate-level thermodynamics (which will be briefly reviewed in the course)</dd>

  <dt>Evaluation</dt>
  <dd>Midterm 40%, final 40%, problem sets 20%</dd>
</dl>
